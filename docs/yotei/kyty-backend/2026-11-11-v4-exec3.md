## 0. Answer

1. **What bounds the frame now** [M]. Standing still at the river village the best build's frame is 115-118 ms
   (86.75 presents/10 s in ve3-burn1's off phases; 84.2 in ve3-tr2 with samplers beside it). It has two parts:
   - **A boundary of ~30 ms**, from the exec thread's flip to the guest's next queue-0 submission, in which the exec
     thread has ~8 ms of work. It is a chain: the GPU finishes the frame's last buffers (the flip is ready 5-9 ms
     after the exec thread passed it); the exec thread waits for the output of the first dispatches after the flip,
     then works ~6 ms; the GPU runs the frame-start compute (~9.4 ms); the exec thread and a guest thread wait for
     it; the guest reads the result and submits queue 0, 21-23 ms after ready. 15-19 ms of it are GPU time
     [ve3-tl1 frames 4001-4002, ve3-tr1, ve3-tr2; §2.6].
   - **A body of ~88 ms that follows the exec thread.** 4 us of added work per draw (12.9 ms per frame) lengthen the
     frame by 11.8-12.4 ms and leave the boundary where it is: 0.91-0.96 ms of frame per ms of work
     [ve3-burn1, fps -9.1 %, SE 0.5; §2.7].
   - The exec thread per frame [ve3-tr2; §2.2]: ~72 ms of per-operation work (7,365 operations, flat profile), ~28 ms
     blocked on the GPU (13-19 of them at the boundary), ~6 ms of readback copies, 4.3 ms of page protection,
     7.5-9.5 ms waiting for an operation.
   - "Exec busy + ~29 ms" still fits as a formula (frame = 21.7 + 1.10 x busy, r 0.84). The ~30 ms are this
     boundary, not guest pacing or label lag, and not removable waits as my interim message said.
2. **Levers** (§3). **No single exact lever of +10 % exists in what I measured.**
   - Next exact step: GPU copies off the exec thread, +5-8 % [E], 3-4 days (design in §3.2).
   - The boundary's round trips (fault requests queueing behind exec operations, late submits): +1-4 % [E] together.
   - Per-operation cost: +6 % per 10 % of it removed [E from the measured slope]; weeks.
   - Together the exact route is +6-12 % [E] in about two weeks.
   - The only single step of +10-15 % [E] is inexact (the boundary without its reads): the user's decision; not built.
3. **Lever 1, built and measured: it stays off.** `KYTY_PERF_VERIFY_RANGE` (new) +2.4 % (SE 1.7),
   `KYTY_PERF_EXACT_WALKER` +2.3 % (SE 1.8), both +2.4 % (SE 2.4) in a 1,500-s in-run A/B [ve3-ab1]; ~0 on the six
   undisturbed cycles. Gates passed: T1 MATCH with both on [ve3-t1on], world run without a fatal. The counters say
   why: asked by exact byte range, the read-log check still waits 0.99 times per frame, so the wait is a true
   dependency (§4).
4. **System process** (§5): threads of the NVIDIA kernel driver, busy only while no game runs; it came with the
   621.14 update that had no reboot. A reboot is the cheap test; whether it cures it is not verified.
5. Not verified: §6. What I leave and what I would do next: §7.

## 2. Where the frame goes

### 2.1 The traced recipe is no longer cheap
- ve3-tr1 runs at 66.9 presents/10 s, ve3-tr2 at 84.2. The only difference is `KYTY_TRACE_SYNC_TIMELINE`.
- Cause [C `syncTimeline.cpp` NoteDownload]: with the sync timeline on, every published download is read back from guest
  memory and compared, to count "changed" bytes. With `KYTY_PERF_BAR_GPU_LARGE` the downloads are 37 MB per frame, so
  the compare is a second pass over all of it on the exec thread (ucrtbase 20.6 % of its samples in tr1 against 11.7 %
  in tr2). On the G2 build the downloads were 3 MB per frame and the trace was free (61.5 traced, 61.8 untraced).
- So: the sync timeline only for its detail frames; times and shares from a run without it. All numbers below are
  ve3-tr2 unless tr1 is named.

### 2.4 What bounds the frame; the "exec busy + ~29 ms" model
- The fit still holds: frame = 21.7 + 1.10 x exec busy (r 0.84), overhead (frame - busy) 30.3 ms, flat across the
  deciles (idle 17.3-19.1 ms for busy 75-109 ms) [M execfr w1].
- But the 30 ms are not what the 11-09 report took them for (guest pacing, label waits, sub-ms idle), and not what my
  interim message of 00:32 took them for (two waits that could be removed). **They are the frame boundary: a chain of
  true dependencies between the GPU, the exec thread and the guest, ~30 ms long, in which the exec thread has
  ~8 ms of work.** §2.6 has its timeline. Time zero below is the exec thread's flip (the FRAMETIME mark), not the
  present.
  - In 5-ms bins after the flip the exec thread is busy 0.03, 0.29, 0.62, 0.21, 0.35 cores (7.5 ms in 25 ms), then
    0.6-0.98; the command processor gets nothing before 25 ms [M tana w1].
  - 14.5 ms of the 30 are two blocking waits in the first 20 ms (1.95 gaps per frame) [M execfr w1]; ~12 ms are blocks
    under 1 ms (the 15 fence waits of the GPU copy are 5.0 ms of them) and partly busy ticks; ~3.7 ms are gaps of 1 ms
    or more later in the frame.
  - The guest's next queue-0 submission comes 23.1 ms after the flip is ready (median 22.2, p10 20.2, p90 25.6), and
    no other queue is submitted before it [M ve3-tr2 HT lines: q0r 23.14, anyr 23.12 ms over frames 3000-6500]. Ready
    is itself 5-9 ms after the exec thread's flip (the GPU's backlog, §2.6).
  - Until then the guest's main thread sits in the poll loop eboot+0x1139580 (99 % of its samples up to 20 ms after the
    flip, 62 % at 25-30 ms; 48 % of all its samples) [M `tools/guestloops.py` w2].
  - In standalone Kyty the main thread is not in that loop at all (its frame-boundary loop is 0x900d92940, which it
    leaves 7-10 ms after the flip), and the first compute submission comes 4 ms after ready [M v4h2-s-tr1 w2]. Why the
    standalone guest starts earlier is not identified.
- The body, from the queue-0 submission to the next flip (~88 ms), is bound by the exec thread: in the timeline frames
  the exec thread waits for the GPU 5.1 and ~9 ms there, and the GPU is busy a quarter of that time [M ve3-tl1].
  An added ms of per-draw work there costs 0.91-0.96 ms of frame [M ve3-burn1, §2.7].
- So the frame is: a ~30-ms boundary that the GPU and the dependency chain bound, plus an ~88-ms body that the exec
  thread bounds. The GPU-copy and LARGE work of 11-09/11-10 shortened the body; the boundary is as it was on G2
  (direct-range wait 8.2 ms, GPU waits 23.8 ms per frame there [M v4h2-k-tr1]).

### 2.7 How much of an added ms of per-draw work arrives in the frame [M ve3-burn1]

- Run: best build, in-run A/B of the diagnostic burn that the DLL already has (`KYTY_DIAG_GPU_BURN_AT=draw`,
  `KYTY_LIVE_AB=KYTY_DIAG_GPU_BURN_NS=4000`): in the on phases the exec thread spins 4 us in every
  `ExecutePreparedDraw`, 3,221 times per frame = **12.9 ms per frame**. 900 s, 30-s phases, 10 cycles, no pad presses,
  no side jobs. Fatal none, RESTORE OK, RUN1080 OK. Driver 621.14.
- Result (`research/v4/e3/logs/ve3-burn1.ab.txt`, `.phaseperf.txt`):

| phase | frame mean / median | presents/10 s | exec busy per frame | waiting for an operation | ready to queue 0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | 115.3 / 113.2 ms | 86.75 | 107.8 ms | 7.5 ms | 21.4 ms |
| burn | 127.0 / 125.6 ms | 78.72 | 120.6 ms | 6.2 ms | 21.4 ms |

- fps -9.1 % (SE 0.5); the frame grows by 11.8 ms (mean) and 12.4 ms (median) for 12.9 ms of added work:
  **0.91-0.96 ms of frame per ms of per-draw work.** The exec thread's waiting for operations takes up 1.2 ms of it.
- The boundary does not move (ready to queue 0: 21.4 ms in both phases). So the model that holds is: a boundary of
  ~30 ms that per-draw work does not change, plus a body that follows the exec thread almost one for one.
- It measures added work. Removing work can gain less once another stage becomes the limit (resolve has ~61 ms of
  real work per frame, §2.3); that side is not measured.
- By the way: the off phases are the cleanest reading of the best build standing still, 86.75 presents/10 s

### 3.2 Lever 2 in more detail (design only, nothing built) [C]
- Today (`BufferCache::ReadMemory` -> `TryBarDownload` -> `BarGpuRead`): the exec thread records and submits the copy on
  the second queue, waits for its fence, copies the staged bytes into guest memory (`WriteBacking`), then changes the
  page states. Everything else waits behind it: the next operation, the other guest threads' fault requests and the
  label publishes.
- Proposed: for a guest thread's fault (the caller-wait case, `PlanCallerWait`), the exec thread takes the intervals
  out of the GPU-dirty set and submits the copy as now, registers the window as a pending readback (the state
  `m_eager_readbacks` already has: `ApplyEagerReadbacks`, `WaitPendingReadbacks`, `HasPendingReadback` and the
  write-through's "pending" decline make every reader of those bytes wait or decline), and hands the request back. A
  helper thread waits for the fence and publishes; the faulting thread waits for that and sends its request again,
  which then finds the window published and only changes the page states.
- Why it stays exact: the published bytes are the ones the copy takes today (same tick, same intervals); the pages stay
  closed to the guest until the second request; a GPU write recorded meanwhile puts its range back into the GPU-dirty
  set, so a stale byte in guest memory is never visible; the exec thread's own readers wait on the pending state as
  they do for an eager readback.
- To check while building it: the copy runs on the second queue beside later work of the main queue (bytes rewritten
  meanwhile are re-marked, see above); several copies in flight need staging slots (today one 16-MiB buffer); the
  resolve thread's ahead reads see the bytes as not GPU-dirty before they are published (the exec thread's check at
  execution time must catch that, as it does for eager readbacks).
- The exec thread's own reads (SRT walker) cannot be handed back; they stay synchronous.

the backing by themselves, and a window whose copy is under way is exactly what an eager readback already is. Every
reader handles that state today: the pages stay GPU-modified and closed, the exec thread's readers wait
(`WaitPendingReadbacks`), the resolve thread sees it as pending, the write-through declines, other faulting threads
wait for the same value. Done by hand, because this copy runs beside the main queue and not in tick order:
- a download whose range overlaps such a window waits for the copy before it publishes (`WaitCopyReadbacks`);
- no second hand-back copy starts over a window that has one under way;
- an eager readback does not unmark a page that holds bytes of an unfinished hand-back copy;
- a finished hand-back readback unmarks its window run by run and keeps pages with newer GPU bytes.

**What it can gain [E, revised].** 2a removes the publish (3.6 ms per frame); the hand-back removes the fence wait of
the copies made for guest faults (~4 of the 5.0 ms per frame; the walker's own copies stay synchronous). Each alone is
~+3 % at the measured slope, both together ~+6 %. The hand-back costs the faulting thread one more round trip to the
exec thread, which can take part of it back.

### 9.5 For the user: "the boundary without its reads" (task 5; nothing built)

**What it is.** At every frame boundary the game waits ~20 ms for results of GPU compute it has just submitted, reads
them on the CPU and only then builds the next frame; the exec thread waits for the same results to resolve the next
dispatches. The exact way waits. The inexact way lets both go on with the bytes guest memory already holds: the
results of the previous frame.

**What it would do on screen.** Whatever the game computes from those results arrives one frame late: 115 ms at
today's 8.7 fps, 33 ms at 30 fps. What the results are is not identified yet (§9.4 is meant to name them); such
readbacks are usually visibility lists, GPU-simulated positions (cloth, particles) or counts for draws [E].

**Where it would be visibly wrong [E].**
- Camera cuts, teleports, the first frame after a load: one frame is built from the previous view's results, so
  objects are missing or extra for that frame (a flash).
- Fast camera turns: objects at the screen edge appear one frame late.
- Standing still or moving slowly: no visible difference expected.
- The larger risk is not the picture: if the game reads a count in one place and a list in another and only one of
  them is stale, it can index past the list. The stale bytes must be one consistent frame, never a mix.

**How the error can be bounded.**
- One snapshot per frame: the previous frame's results are published whole at the end of the frame, so a read sees
  one consistent state that is exactly one frame old.
- A change detector: once the GPU has finished this frame's results they are compared with the snapshot; when more
  than a set number of bytes differ, the next frame waits the exact way. A cut then costs one wrong frame, not more,
  and every such frame is counted.
- For the exec thread's own reads: go on only where the last frames' bytes were identical, check when the GPU has
  finished, count mismatches (both such reads in ve3-tl1 had the verdict "same").
- T1 cannot stay byte-exact with it. Its gate would be an image-difference bound against the exact build on recorded
  input, to be agreed with the user.

**Before deciding: a shadow counter (exact, ~2 days).** It changes nothing and counts, per frame and per scene
(standing, walking, camera turns, a cutscene), how many bytes of these reads differ between the previous frame's
results and this frame's. That is the honest size of the error, measured before anything inexact exists.

**Gain [E].** The boundary is ~30 ms of a 115-ms frame. Without the waits the game could submit queue 0 some 4-10 ms
after ready, as standalone Kyty's guest does, instead of 21-23 ms: 12-17 ms per frame, **+11-17 %**. Not verified;
the GPU still has to run the compute and the frame's last buffers, and if the next frame's work queues behind them
part of the gain is lost.

**Prototype cost.** Shadow counter 2 days. Prototype behind an opt-in switch 4-6 days (snapshot at frame end, the
fault served from it, the exec thread's reads with their check, the change detector), plus 1-2 days to measure image
differences on recorded input. Suggested stop rule: under +8 %, or any crash.

### 10.1 Why the hand-back mismatches (task 1)

**What the check does** [C d659f9d6 `VerifyCopyReadback`]. When a finished hand-back readback is applied
(`ApplyEagerReadbacks`), each of its intervals is compared byte for byte: guest memory against the GPU buffer's mapping
*at that moment*. Intervals that are GPU-dirty again are skipped (a GPU write was recorded over them since the copy).
Nothing else is skipped. In particular, bytes the *CPU* changed since the copy are not skipped. The synchronous copy's
check (`BarGpuRead`) compares at once, right after the fence, and found 0 of 484 copies wrong in the same run. The copy
mechanism is the same (2a's copy into the backing), so the difference comes from what happens between landing and
applying.

**How the pages of a landed copy are released before it is applied** [C]. The hand-back keeps the window's pages
GPU-modified (closed to the game) until the readback is applied. But several release paths treat a readback that has
*landed* (its `done` flag set, which `EagerDone`, `WaitCopyReadbacks` and the joiners set) as no longer holding pages:
- `UnmarkWindow` (another fault's synchronous read-window download): skips only readbacks that are not done. Its
  `DownloadBufferMemory` first waits for overlapping hand-back copies (`WaitCopyReadbacks`), which sets `done`. Then it
  unmarks the whole window, the landed copy's pages included. That fault applied only the readbacks that overlap its
  own access (`ApplyEagerReadbacks(vaddr, size)`), not the ones in the rest of its megabyte window.
- `UnmarkPublishedWindow` (another hand-back applied first) and the eager loop (`PendingReadbackCovers`): the same,
  they keep only unfinished readbacks.

When this happens the release is correct. The bytes are in guest memory and are not GPU-dirty, so "GPU-modified page
<=> bytes to download" holds. But from then on the game can write those pages without faulting. The data ring
(0x2002600000-0x2010000000) is rewritten by the game every second frame. Its new CPU bytes reach the GPU buffer only
at the next upload, so a check that runs before that upload sees guest memory ≠ mapping. The copy was right and the
check compared at the wrong moment.

**What the 11 cases have in common** [M `research/v4/e4/logs/ve3-cv1.timeline.txt`]:
- **All in the data ring**, in two clusters: 0x2003ce6000-0x20041e1dc0 (5 cases) and 0x200af15970-0x200b96e000
  (6 cases). These are ring pages that the dead-ring rule (`DropDeadRingBytes`) also handles.
- **Frames 822-998 are the world, not the load.** Hand-back copies begin at frame ~717. Up to frame 817 there were
  226 copies with 4,968 verified intervals and no mismatch. From 817 there were 2,536 copies with 118,643 verified
  intervals and 12 mismatches. At the world's rate (~1 per 10,000 intervals) the load would show 0.5, so "none in the
  load" means nothing. The run ends at frame 1,011, so the 11 are spread evenly over the world frames (one per ~17
  frames). Frame 817 is where the game's heavy ring rewriting starts: the dead-ring rule frees 1,473 pages in the 5-s
  window at frame 817 and 100-1,100 per window after it, against 2-68 before.
- **Large intervals:** 0.3-3.1 MB each, against a mean of ~60 KB (7.1 GB in 118,643 intervals). A hit by a few
  stray bytes scales with size, which fits small CPU writes landing somewhere in a released window.
- **The 12th mismatch:** the per-call log prints only the first differing interval of a readback (`if (mismatched++ ==
  0 && ...)`), and the sum counts intervals. So one of the 11 readbacks had two differing intervals. No synchronous
  copy mismatched (0 `PERF_BAR_GPU_COPY_MISMATCH` lines; their log limit of 16 was not reached).

**What reading cannot exclude:** a real wrong byte. One such hole is in the code, though nothing shows it happened:
- `CpWriteThroughOwnedPage` (a command-processor write that replaces GPU bytes in an owned page) unmarks the page
  when no GPU-dirty byte is left. It does not ask `PendingReadbackCovers` for the *page*, only for the written bytes.
- If a hand-back copy is still under way in that page, the game could read the page before the copy lands (old
  bytes), or write it and then have the copy overwrite its write.
- This needs a ≤256-byte command-processor write over bytes the GPU wrote after the copy started, in a ring page with
  a copy in flight. That should be rare, and it also applies to ordinary eager readbacks.
- The fix is one condition: `&& !PendingReadbackCovers(page, TRACKER_PAGE_SIZE)` at the unmark (bufferCache.cpp ~1745).

**The diagnostic run that settles it** (one diagnostic commit, ~1-2 hours, default off; one 15-minute run):
- **Code:**

    `PERF_COPY_HANDBACK_MISMATCH2 addr size frame diff_bytes first_off pages_diff pages_closed pages_open_cpu
    pages_open_clean`. A page is "closed" when it is still GPU-modified in the tracker at the check; it is "open"

  - Count `strict_mismatched`: differing bytes in closed pages only.
  - In `UnmarkWindow`, `UnmarkPublishedWindow`, the eager loop and `CpWriteThroughOwnedPage`, count by site the pages

### 10.2 Is the hand-back worth fixing? (task 2)

- **What it gains** [M ve3-ab2, 1,800 s standing still, 10 cycles]: +3.0 % (SE 0.6), median frame 114.3 → 110.9 ms.

  - What it costs back: faulting threads resend their request (deferrals 1.4 → 34.3 per frame, `read_memory` requests
    68 → 91 per frame); 121 per frame are declined (each one plans and scans); guest threads wait for copies
    themselves (caller waits 1.9 → 36 ms per frame, summed over threads).

    queue 0: 21.05 → 21.95 ms median), so the gain is in the frame's body.
- **If the check was at fault:** fix the check (VERIFY=2 plus the strict count, part of the diagnostic commit) and add
  the one-line guard in `CpWriteThroughOwnedPage`. Gates: a clean verify run and T1 (T1 makes no GPU copy).

  still and a pad-recipe run, since +3.0 sits on the line. **The gain is unchanged:** nothing in the path changes.
- **If the run finds a real race:**
  - *An early release, or the command-processor hole:* the exact fix is "a hand-back readback's pages stay closed
    until it is *applied*, not until it has *landed*". In `UnmarkWindow`, `UnmarkPublishedWindow` and the eager loop
    that means asking "not yet applied" instead of `done`, and in the command-processor write, asking for the page.
    That costs half a day and adds no wait: a fault on such a page applies the landed readback at once (it no longer
    waits). The gain should survive (−0.5 % at most [E], from a few more applies per frame).
  - *The copy itself racing later GPU writes:* nothing in the evidence points there; every write recorded after the
    copy marks its bytes GPU-dirty again. The exact fix would be to order the copy after the main queue's newest
    recorded work. That delays each copy by the GPU's backlog (5-20 ms), so guest threads wait longer. I would expect

- **A follow-on, not worth it alone:** the hand-back's copies go into guest memory at 2a's slow speed (§10.3).
  Copying through the staging buffer, with the faulting thread doing the memcpy on its own time, would cut the guest
  threads' waits (36 ms per frame summed). +0-2 % [E], and it needs staging slots: under +3 % on its own.

### 10.6 For the user: "the frame boundary without its reads" (half a page; nothing built)

**What happens today.** At the start of every frame the game first has the graphics card do a batch of calculations
(most of it a cloth simulation, ~6.5 ms). Then one of the game's threads reads the results back, works on them for
about 5 ms, and only after that sends the next frame's main work. The emulator also has to read a few of those results
before it can continue preparing graphics commands. Both wait, and the frame start costs ~20-30 ms of each ~115-ms
frame in which little else happens.

**The inexact idea.** At that point, let the game and the emulator read the *previous frame's* results, which are
already in memory, instead of waiting for this frame's. The real results are still computed. They are just used one
frame later.

**What it would look like on screen.** Whatever the game derives from those results would lag by one frame: 115 ms at
today's speed, 33 ms at 30 fps. If it is the cloth, as the trace suggests, capes and banners would trail the body
slightly during fast movement, which is probably invisible when standing or walking. If some of it is visibility
(which objects to draw), objects could appear or vanish one frame late. Where it would be visibly wrong:
- camera cuts, teleports and the first frame after a loading screen: one frame built from the previous scene's
  results, a one-frame flash;
- very fast camera turns: something at the screen edge one frame late;
- the worst case is a crash rather than a picture error: the game could read a count from one frame and a list from
  another.

**How the error would be bounded and detected.**
- Use one consistent copy of the previous frame's results, never a mix, so every read sees one whole older frame.
- When the real results arrive, compare them with the old ones and count the bytes that changed. Above a threshold
  (a cut, a load), the next frame waits the exact way, so a cut costs at most one wrong frame, and every such frame is
  counted in the log.
- The usual byte-exact picture check (T1) can no longer pass. The gate would be a picture-difference limit against
  the exact build on recorded input, to be agreed with you.
- First, a two-day **shadow counter** that changes nothing: it only measures, standing, walking, turning and in a
  cutscene, how much these results really change from one frame to the next. That is the honest size of the error
  before anything inexact exists.

**Expected gain.** +8-15 % [E] (v4e3 said up to +10-15 %). It is not more, because the graphics card must still do the
same calculations and the rest of the frame still follows the emulator's main thread. The game's own 4-7 ms of work
after its read would partly overlap the next frame instead of sitting in front of it.

**Cost.** Shadow counter 2 days. Prototype behind an opt-in switch 4-6 days, plus 1-2 days to measure picture
differences on recorded input: about 1.5 weeks. Suggested stop rule: under +8 %, any crash, or a visible error outside
cuts.
