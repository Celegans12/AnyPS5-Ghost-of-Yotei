# relinker

## add Functional

- An Intel conversion stub that took in the instruction after the one it lowers, such as the 4-byte VRCPPS or VRSQRTPS the conversion widens over the next instruction, leaves that instruction outside the reach of `--frame-red-zone`; the framer moves the displacement of an RSP operand it holds inside the stub body ([framer](../../../core/relinker/codegen/src/x86/RedZoneFramer.cpp)) only when the moved displacement keeps its width. A displacement that must grow, a stub that took in a push, a pop or an instruction without a displacement, an operand of the lowered instruction itself and a leaf that has to be relocated whole over any stub are not framed: the leaf goes into the list of unframed ranges. Of three titles converted with `--to-intel`, 8 of 9, 6 of 7 and 8 of 19 leaves that overlapped a stub are framed now; none runs framed.
- The list of unframed ranges of an image ([marking](../../../core/relinker/relinker/src/pipeline/RedZoneFraming.cpp)) is a `.rzgap` section appended to the PE after it is written, and the mark of the image becomes `APS5RZ02` with the table's RVA at 0x30 and its entry count at 0x34. It has one entry per range, merged where they touch: an image without room for another section header, with a mark or table bytes already in use or with 96 sections is left unmarked. Each range is a whole function, as the original ELF lays it out: a fault inside the body of an Intel stub that belongs to a listed leaf runs from the stub area, outside the range, so the gate does not see it; the stub ends with the instruction it took in, after the leaf's red zone is restored, and the leaves left in a list are the ones that hold such a stub. A leaf entered from another function's range, or whose red zone a callee reaches, is not covered more finely.

# libc

## add Functional

- The [red-zone gate](../../../core/libs/prx/libc/include/RedZoneGate.hpp) (libc) on Windows keeps page tracking on for an image marked `APS5RZ02` and stops the title with a message when a fault that page tracking served has its RIP inside a range of the image's list ([crash handler](../../../core/libs/prx/libc/src/specifics/windows/CrashReport.cpp)); by then Windows has written the exception record over the leaf's red zone, so the stop replaces a silent corruption, it does not prevent one. The RIP is read before the server runs, since a server that steps over the faulting instruction moves it out of the range. The handler that libkernel adds in front of the crash handler for guest signals serves the read and write traps of watched views too, so it asks libc for the same check (`LibcUnframedFaultSite_nid_no_patch` before the trap server, `LibcRefuseUnframedFault_nid_no_patch` after it served). It reads at most 32 tables, one per image, and treats an image with more, or with a table that is not sorted, overlaps itself or leaves the image, as unframed. Its sources compile for Windows with MinGW g++ (syntax and warnings, no link) and its table and fault-site logic ran on Linux against a shim of the Windows types; the fault path has not run on Windows.
